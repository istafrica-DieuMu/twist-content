## Structure

### Official areas per subject

**Erdkunde / Geographie**

- DGfG (national, by the subject association): six Kompetenzbereiche,
  Fachwissen (F), Räumliche Orientierung (O), Erkenntnisgewinnung/Methoden
  (M), Kommunikation (K), Beurteilung/Bewertung (B), Handlung (H)
  (rnd/official-de/dgfg/text/Bildungsstandards-SI-Geographie-2020-Web.txt:435-447;
  standards F1 at 617, O1 to O5 at 764 to 818, M at 897, K at 983, B at
  1067, H at 1185). It sets competences, not topics or years.
- NRW G9 Kernlehrplan Erdkunde (nrw/text/g9_ek_klp_3408_2019_06_23.txt):
  Erprobungsstufe (end of Klasse 6) with Inhaltsfelder 1 Unterschiedlich
  strukturierte Siedlungen, 2 Räumliche Voraussetzungen und Auswirkungen
  des Tourismus, 3 Arbeit und Versorgung in Wirtschaftsräumen
  unterschiedlicher Ausstattung (lines 611 to 700); end of Sek I with 4
  Aufbau und Dynamik der Erde, 5 Wetter und Klima, 6 Landwirtschaftliche
  Produktion in unterschiedlichen Landschaftszonen, 7 Innerstaatliche und
  globale Disparitäten, 8 Wachstum und Verteilung der Weltbevölkerung, 9
  Verstädterung und Stadtentwicklung, 10 Räumliche Strukturen unter dem
  Einfluss von Globalisierung und Digitalisierung (lines 848 to 1085).
- Bayern LehrplanPLUS Gymnasium Geographie (by/text/lehrplanplus-gym-geographie-jgs*.txt):
  Jgst 5 Planet Erde; Naturräume, ländliche und städtische Räume in Bayern
  und Deutschland (jgs5:39-44). Jgst 7 Europa: Einheit und Vielfalt,
  Naturgeographie, ländliche Räume, Metropolen, Meere und Küsten
  (jgs7:39-45). Jgst 10 Leben in der Einen Welt, Tropen und aride
  Subtropen, Klima im Wandel, Nordafrika und Naher Osten, Afrika südlich
  der Sahara, Lateinamerika (jgs10:39-45). Every year opens with
  Lernbereich 1 Geographische Arbeitstechniken. Pages for Jgst 6, 8 and 9
  did not resolve; check whether G9 Bayern teaches Geographie there.

Twist places each skill by the Gymnasium order of NRW and Bayern. Where
they differ, the earlier year wins if the skill needs nothing from later
years; civics starts in Klasse 6 (end of the NRW Erprobungsstufe) and its
institutional core sits in 8, so a child in a Land that starts later meets
it when the profile asks for it.

### Twist domains and progressions

Three domains. Geography and history are separate subjects in every Land
and in England. Politics and economics are combined in NRW
(Wirtschaft-Politik), Niedersachsen (Politik-Wirtschaft) and Hessen
(Politik und Wirtschaft), and split in Bayern and BW; one domain `civics`
with an `economy` progression fits both, and each curriculum can name the
subject as it likes (`subjects: { civics: Politik und Wirtschaft }`). en-GB
can map `civics` to Citizenship. A separate `economics` domain would leave
NRW's single subject spread over two domains.

| Progression | Content | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `geography.orientation` | maps, atlas, coordinates, topography, diagrams, analysis | `world.space` |
| `geography.physical` | Earth, relief, tectonics, weather, climate, zones | `world.space`, `world.nature` |
| `geography.human` | settlement, farming, industry, services, tourism, cities | `world.space`, `world.society` |
| `geography.global` | development, population, migration, climate change, globalisation | new |
| `history.method` | sources, dating, maps, judgement, remembrance | `world.time` |
| `history.ancient` | prehistory to the Franks | `world.time` |
| `history.medieval` | about 900 to 1500 | `world.time.middle-ages` |
| `history.early-modern` | 1450 to 1750 | new |
| `history.modern` | 1750 to 1918 | new |
| `history.contemporary` | 1918 to today | `world.time.divided-germany` |
| `civics.society` | identity, family, media, living together, inequality | `world.society` |
| `civics.democracy` | participation, Grundgesetz, institutions, elections | `world.society.democracy-vote`, `municipality` |
| `civics.law` | rights of minors, contracts, criminal law, rule of law | `world.society.children-rights` |
| `civics.economy` | household, market, companies, work, social security | `world.society.pocket-money`, `advertising` |
| `civics.world` | EU, UN, human rights, peace, global economy | `world.society.germany-eu` |

History runs in chronological order through Klasse 6 to 10, as in both
Länder. `history.method` skills sit in the year where a source type first
matters.

### Levels

- **ESA**: the topic is in the NRW Hauptschule Gesellschaftslehre plan
  (Erdkunde, Geschichte, Politik). Economy items marked ESA are a judgment
  (Hauptschule Wirtschaft not captured); they carry "check".
- **MSA**: taught in Realschule and Gymnasium; absent from or only touched
  in the Hauptschule plan, or needs more abstraction.
- **G**: only in the Gymnasium plans, or beyond what the MSA needs.
- **later in RS**: not set. The NRW Realschule plans captured (Erdkunde,
  Politik) use the same two stages as the Gymnasium and give no year-level
  evidence; check against a Realschule plan with year bands (Bayern
  Realschule) before marking.

### Present-day politics and the Beutelsbacher Konsens

The Beutelsbacher Konsens (captured: bpb/text/bpb-beutelsbacher-konsens.txt:177-182)
has three rules: no overwhelming (Überwältigungsverbot), what is
controversial in science and politics appears as controversial, and the
learner is enabled to analyse a situation and their own interests. Twist
lessons keep to it like this:

1. **Mastery counts knowledge and analysis, never opinion.** A task may ask
   which institution decides, which argument belongs to which side, which
   interest stands behind a demand, or whether an argument is a fact claim
   or a value claim. A task never marks a political position right or
   wrong. Judgement tasks (`political-judgement`, `history.method.judgement`)
   check that a reason fits the criterion, as in ../ethik.md.
2. **Every controversial question shows at least two positions** with
   their strongest arguments, sourced, in equal length and tone. Authors
   take positions from the range represented in the Bundestag and in
   science, not invented straw men.
3. **Fictional actors for current controversies.** Simulations use made-up
   parties, towns and politicians. Real parties appear in history (Weimar,
   NSDAP, SED, the founding of the Federal Republic) and when the lesson
   explains how parties work in general, never with current programmes.
4. **What is not controversial.** Human dignity, the free democratic basic
   order, the rejection of National Socialism, the facts of the Holocaust
   and of the GDR dictatorship, and the scientific consensus on human-made
   climate change are taught as settled, in line with the Grundgesetz and
   the plans (NRW Geschichte IF8 asks to place human rights in their
   universal validity, g9_ge_klp line 1116 ff.). How to respond to climate
   change (`climate-action`, `climate-policy`) is controversial and follows
   rule 2.
5. **Dated facts are data, not text.** Bundestag seats, EU membership,
   minimum wage, contribution rates and similar figures live in one data
   file per curriculum with a date, reviewed each year. Lessons say "im Jahr
   X" rather than "heute".
6. **Review.** Each civics and contemporary-history lesson set gets a
   second review for balance by a reviewer not involved in writing it;
   tracking.md records it as a review pass.
7. **Post-1990 history stops before ongoing events.** `world-after-1990`
   names actors and institutions; wars and crises still going on are not
   lesson content.

### Sensitive topics

The Holocaust, the war of annihilation, colonial genocide, slavery,
witch-hunts and the Stasi get no humour, no funny wrong options, no
celebration animation after the lesson and no time pressure. The mastery
check stays; the child is told before the lesson what it is about. Photos
of victims are not used; sources are texts, documents and maps. Wording
follows the Gedenkstätten and bpb practice (check against a bpb guide
before authoring).
