Handoff · Schoolido Twist

Twist curriculum handoff

The new curriculum model from R&D, and what it asks of the backend API (IST-B2C/backend), the mobile app (ist-schoolido) and the parent dashboard (IST-B2C/Parent-dashboard).

Source rnd repo, branch main, folder twist/ Written 13 October 2026, updated 16 October 2026 (recaps) Content status every placed skill drafted; model-reviewed, not yet teacher-reviewed

TL;DR

  • New chain. Grade, Subject, Unit, Lesson, Module, Step, Question becomes Curriculum, Subject, Year, Topic, Skill, Lesson, Activity, Task. The skill becomes the unit of progress: mastery, recaps, placement and parent reporting all count skills, not modules.
  • Two curricula, one shared skill library. de-DE (KMK Bildungsstandards) and en-GB (EYFS and the National Curriculum in England), built from 7 official documents and 697 quoted standards. de-DE places 435 skills, en-GB 483, and 257 skills are shared between them.
  • Three fields where there is one today. Year sets what the curriculum expects, the child's level on each learning progression sets where they work, and age sets how a lesson looks. Today one integer gradeLevel does all three.
  • Every lesson has the same four activities. Warm-up, Learn, Practise (three stages with hints fading from 3 to 1 to none) and Check. A skill reaches Can do it after a 4-of-5 skill check and Remembers it after spaced recaps; there are six states.
  • All of it is drafted. Every placed skill in both curricula has a lesson set: 918 lesson sets, 1,892 lessons and 40,180 tasks (de-DE 435 sets and 19,503 tasks, en-GB 483 sets and 20,677 tasks). Model agents have reviewed every set; no teacher has yet.
  • Player. The tasks use 17 task types. At the 13 October read of the app, 10 of them render. audio_choice, sorting and speak have components that the question mapper drops. highlight_in_text, drag_and_drop_count, clock_hands_drag and point_on_grid have no component. text_input now has four keyboards (digits, decimal, fraction, letters). 43 drawn components (dots, ten-frame, clock, coins, protractor, coordinates, bar chart, maps of Germany and the world, a road junction, a circuit, the water cycle, a timeline …) are drawn from data; the app draws only the number line.
  • Audio. A character's line names speaker: male or female and a dialogue is a list of turns, each in its speaker's voice. The voice reads maths symbols as words (7/5 as "7 fifths", −4 as "negative 4"); any text-to-speech or clip lookup in the app must use the same spoken form.
  • Dashboard. Grade pills (Pre-K, Grade 1 to 12) become curriculum plus year (Klasse 1 to 4, Reception and Year 1 to 6), with a foreign-language learning year. Module-percentage status ("On track", "Gap identified", "Week behind") becomes standing per subject (ahead, on track, catching up) from skills at Can do it, plus a skill list with states. 18 skills are enrichment (no standard of the curriculum asks for them) and can be shown as "extra".
  • Content source. Lessons are hand-written YAML records that point at an asset bank by id. No converter to the backend importer's JSON exists yet, and the importer today turns any unknown task type into multiple_choice without failing.

Run the lesson player

  1. Get the repo. git clone git@github.com:istafrica-DieuMu/twist-content.git and cd twist-content.
  2. Open it. Open twist/pages/player/index.html in Chrome or Safari, straight from disk; no server is needed. It loads the pictures and narration from twist/assets/ in the same clone. In the panel next to the phone, search or filter by curriculum, subject, year and task type, then tap a lesson number; tap through it as a child would. The arrow keys step through a lesson, and with a task type chosen, "Next" jumps to its next task. The screen follows the app: header with lesson menu and step badge, the mascot's speech bubble, select then Prüfen, and the result sheet.
  3. The voice. The narration clips are in git (about 3 GB), so a clone plays with sound. To make clips for new or changed lines you need Python 3.9 or later and an OpenRouter key: 
    python3 -m venv twist/.venv
twist/.venv/bin/pip install -r twist/assets/tools/requirements.txt
OPENROUTER_API_KEY=… twist/.venv/bin/python twist/assets/tools/narration.py --voice all
    It makes every missing clip, checks each one by listening, and skips clips that already exist, so it can be rerun at any time.
  4. After a lesson record changes. With Node 18 or later: cd twist && npm install && cd .. once, then node twist/build.mjs (checks every record), run narration as above for the new lines, then node twist/pages/player/build.mjs to rebuild twist/pages/player/index.html. The player build stops if a spoken line has no entry in twist/assets/narration/lines.yaml; it only reports audio files that are missing on your machine.

Grade determines curriculum expectations, proficiency determines learning level, and age determines presentation. The child's year says what the curriculum expects by the end of the year. Their level on each learning progression says where they actually work, above or below their year. Their age says how a lesson looks and sounds. Keep these as three fields.

Ids

  • Ids never contain a grade or year, because the same skill sits in different years in different curricula. Skill: math.numbers-to-20.compare-to-10. Lesson set: de-DE.math.compare-to-10. Task: l1-f3, unique inside its lesson set.
  • Lowercase ASCII, umlauts written out (ä as ae). Once published an id never changes; a rename is a new id with a replaces field.

Practise stages

StageHintsOn a miss
full3The next hint opens: a strategy, then thinking aloud, then a direct cue
some1The one hint opens
none0After two misses the answer is shown, and the task counts as neither right nor wrong

Tasks also get harder across the stages: the full stage starts easy and shows the drawn model, and by the none stage the model is often gone and the answer is typed rather than chosen. Between stages the player shows a short marker ("Now with less help").

Rules every lesson follows (the rnd build checks them)

  • Anything new gets a Learn before the first Practise. A lesson never ends on Learn.
  • The last lesson of a skill ends with the skill check. A skill check never repeats a task from the lessons word for word.
  • Tracing tasks take no text hints; the trace guide is the help.
  • speak tasks are practice only and never in a skill check, because speech recognition for young children is not reliable enough to mark.
  • A lesson marked no_reading (Stufe 0, ages 4 to 5) has no task where the child must read; instructions are spoken and options are pictures, sounds or numbers that say themselves.
  • In Stufe 0 and the first French lessons, instructions are in the home language and only the target words are in the new one.

Between lessons

  • Each lesson can be finished and left. The next lesson opens straight away.
  • When a skill has more than two lessons, the app suggests the next one on a later day, so the skill spreads over several sittings.

Mastery and what comes next

Mastery is stored per child and per skill. Today the backend stores status and 0 to 3 stars per module from the score percentage (EnumStudentModuleStatus), and the app passes a step at 50 % correct.

API keyChild sees (EN / DE)Parent sees (EN / DE)Reached by
not_startedNot started / Noch nicht begonnenNot started / Noch nicht begonnenNo evidence yet
learningLearning it / Lerne ich geradeLearning / Lernt geradeOpening lesson 1
practisedPractised / GeübtPractised / GeübtFinishing the last lesson below the pass mark
can_doI can do it / Kann ichCan do it / Kann esPassing the skill check, or a placement check
rememberedI still know it / Kann ich sicherRemembers it / Kann es sichercan_do, then the last spaced recap and one earlier recap passed (gaps by age, below)
needs_refreshLet's practise again / Nochmal übenNeeds a refresh / AuffrischenFailing a recap of a can_do or remembered skill. Passing the next recap restores the earlier state.

The keys in the first column (not_started, can_do, remembered, needs_refresh and so on) are the API keys, also in twist/docs/lesson-anatomy.md. The child sees "I" statements that match each skill's "I can ..." line; parents see the third person. Earlier drafts of this page called them secure, mastered and needs_recap.

Skill check

  • 5 tasks for ages 6 and up, 4 for ages 4 to 5, covering every part of the skill. No hints, first try only, a shown answer counts as wrong.
  • Pass: at least 4 of 5 (3 of 4 for ages 4 to 5), and every core task of the skill answered correctly at least once in its lessons.
  • On a fail the skill stays Practised. The app offers one short extra lesson (Learn in a different way, then Practise with full support) and a new skill check with different tasks, drawn from the skill's no-help Practise tasks (each skill has one written skill check). The child never repeats all the lessons.

Recaps

AgesRecaps after can_doremembered when
4 to 51 day, 4 days, 10 days10-day recap and one earlier recap passed
6 to 72 days, 1 week, 2 weeks2-week recap and one earlier recap passed
8 to 102 days, 1 week, 3 weeks3-week recap and one earlier recap passed
  • A recap is 2 tasks (ages 4 to 5) or 2 or 3 tasks of one skill; pass 2 of 2 or 2 of 3. A fail sets needs_refresh, keeps the stars and brings the next recap closer. Upkeep: one more recap 2 months after remembered. Today the backend has one rule: modules completed more than 7 days ago get a recommendation.
  • Warm-up first. When a lesson whose warm-up is from: earlier-skills starts and a builds_on skill has a recap due or is needs_refresh, the warm-up plays that skill's recap tasks in place of the written ones. With nothing due, the written warm-up plays. from: last-lesson warm-ups are never replaced. About two thirds of skills are built on by a later one.
  • Then practice sessions. Due recaps not taken by a warm-up go into the next practice session or topic recap. After a lesson, offer a short session when a recap is overdue; never block.
  • Counts as the recap once 80% of its gap has passed; earlier answers are evidence only.
  • Recap tasks come from the skill's no-help Practise and check tasks (about 18), least recently seen first, never the child's last skill-check tasks, options shuffled.

Placement check (Einstufung)

  • Per learning progression, 1 or 2 tasks per skill. It starts at what the child's year expects, moves up after success and down after mistakes, and stops at the edge.
  • A skill passed there (2 of 2, or 1 of 1 for ages 4 to 5) becomes can_do and gets its first recap scheduled. Earlier skills in the same progression count as can_do too. The first skill missed is the next one to learn.
  • The backend has placement fields on EntityStudentBaselineResult, but nothing writes them and the app has no placement UI.

Suggestions, level and standing

  • Suggested next: a skill whose prerequisites are all at least Practised. A child can still open any skill; if a prerequisite is not yet at can_do, the first lesson starts with a short recap of it.
  • Level in a learning progression: the furthest skill reached with everything it builds on at can_do or better.
  • Standing in a subject, for parents: the share of the child's year's skills at can_do or remembered, against how far the school year has run. Shown as ahead, on track or catching up. It never blocks the child.

What counts for mastery

CountsDoes not count
Skill checks, first tryQuick checks at the end of a lesson
Warm-up tasks that recall a skill already at can_do (as its recap once due)Practise tasks inside lessons
Recaps in warm-ups, practice sessions and topic recapsAnswers shown after two misses
Placement checks; start-of-year and end-of-year checksSpeed, except in a timed skill check

Practice outside lessons comes in four forms: a practice session (about 14 tasks of one skill or a mix of due recaps), a topic recap, a placement check, and a start-of-year or end-of-year check across the whole subject.

All thresholds (check length, pass marks, recap spacing, the remembered rule, placement rule, time targets) are starting values. They belong in app configuration per age, not in the content, and get tuned from real data.

Stars and badges

Every badge, its condition and its English and German name are on their own page: twist/pages/rewards/index.html, built from twist/docs/rewards.md. What the teams need from it:

  • Stars: 0 to 3 per skill from its state: practised 1, can_do 2, remembered 3. needs_refresh keeps the stars and adds a refresh mark. This replaces the 0 to 3 stars per module from the score percentage.
  • Badges: 14 strand badges (one per strand and year, all skills of the strand in that year at can_do), milestones, remembering, streaks, effort and curiosity. A badge is never taken away, levels up each year and turns gold when every skill behind it is remembered.
  • Art: twist/assets/badges/<id>.svg and <id>.gold.svg, 32 badges. The app adds the level chip, the name and the not-yet-earned look; see twist/assets/README.md (Badges).
  • Strands group library areas, the second part of a skill id, so both curricula share them.
  • Streaks count school days: Monday to Friday without the public and school holidays of the child's region. Weekends never break a streak.
  • Only what counts for mastery earns a star or a badge. Levels and day counts are starting values for configuration.

Practice

The rules are in twist/docs/practice.md and as plain, tested functions in twist/pages/player/practice-rules.mjs; the lesson player's Practice tab runs them for a pretend child. What the teams need:

  • Sessions: recaps due, catch-up, topic review, skill practice, drill. 10, 14 or 16 tasks by age; drills run 2, 3 or 5 minutes. Hints are off except one in catch-up. A first-try miss comes back once.
  • Check pools (pilot on the times tables 5 and 10, 3 6 9 and 7): a check with draw holds more tasks than one attempt shows, 20 for a skill check with a part on each task, 6 for a quick check. Each attempt shows 5 (or 3), one of every part, leaving out the last attempt (drawCheck in practice-rules.mjs). Import the whole pool and the set's metadata.parts.
  • Tasks come from the lesson Practise stages and checks, least recently seen first, never the child's last skill-check tasks; while a skill's recap is due, other sessions leave its no-help and check tasks to the recap. Drill items come from twist/drills/<domain>/<skill>.yaml; a foreign-language bank plays only the words of the language the child learns. Wrong options are near misses (nearMisses: 7 × 8 offers 48 and 64; door offers floor and poor).
  • Drill map: "Drill" opens a map of the bank first, a grid for number facts and a wall for words, coloured known, slow, not yet right, not tried. It offers the tricky ones, facts the child taps, or the whole bank. Store per item the times seen, the times right first time, the last result and the day (drillMap, practice.md "Drill map").
  • Scoring: no stars. The child sees the number right (from 8: first-try share too), drills show items per minute and a personal best. Record first try, time and shown answers per task.
  • Tabs: Start (hello and the streak; each subject's next lesson as a big card in a deck that turns by a swipe, a dot a subject under it; then the practice suggestions as tiles in a sideways row), Lernen / Study (subject, year, search and topic chips, then each topic's units and lessons in course order), Üben (practice: suggestions, subjects, games and Own session; Race opens the drills) and Ich (stars, streak, Busy Bee, streak badges, drill records), as the app's Home, Study, Practice and Profile. The tab bar hides inside a lesson or session. A lesson finished from Lernen moves its skill on (finishLesson); the streak counts school days with a lesson or session, weekends neither break nor add (streak).
  • Practice tab: the title, "Für dich" (up to three suggestions as a deck, each with its subject, what it plays and "Start practice"), "Practise a subject" (a tile a subject with a bar of units done; a tile opens Own session on it), "Practice games" (Race: the sprints; Quiz me: a no-hints review of three learnt skills; Surprise: three learnt skills at random, at once), and an "Eigene Runde" / "Own session" button over the page. Own session is two screens: what (a search through every year, from parseRequest now and a language model later; subject chips, then the learnt skills as rows, "Needs practice" above "You know these", as many as the child likes, from several subjects) and how long (5, 10 or 15 minutes, then Let's go). No hints switch. The app's recommendations already carry type, reason, priority and expiry.
  • Typed requests become filter JSON (subject, topics, skills, kind, minutes). The player reads them with keywords; the backend can swap in a language model behind the same contract.
  • App today: practice sessions award nothing and post nothing; answer checking depends on the server sending the correct answer separately (services/quiz/practiceAdapter.ts strips it from the options).

Enrichment

Every placed skill maps to at least one standard of its own curriculum's frameworks, or is listed in that curriculum file's enrichment: map as skill id: reason. The build fails otherwise. de-DE has 3 enrichment skills (written division and its remainders, which some Länder such as Bavaria still teach in Klasse 4; Roman numerals). en-GB has 15 (among them tangrams, chance and fair games, number codes, misleading charts, making charts with a digital tool, typing, and where French is spoken). The backend should store the map with the placement; the dashboard may label these skills "extra", and the standing calculation can leave them out or count them, a product choice.
